Before your consultation

01

Do I need to decide at the consultation?

No. Use the conversation to ask questions and understand your options. Take the information away and decide in your own time.

02

What can I discuss in a consultation?

Your goals, medical history and questions. Your clinician will explain the options, alternatives, recovery and risks, including when they would not recommend treatment. You can request an in-clinic or video consultation; an in-clinic assessment may still be needed.

03

Can I ask about stem cell treatments?

Yes. Start with the exact proposal: which cells are involved, the intended use, the evidence, the risks and the local regulatory status. This demo provides general information and makes no claim that a treatment is approved, safe or effective.

04

Does this demo send or save my details?

No. The form only previews a consultation request on screen. It does not send or save your details, book an appointment or start a video call.

Consultation guide

Your first aesthetic consultation: what to expect

You can start with questions, even if you haven't chosen a treatment. Here's what to prepare and what to discuss, in clinic or by video call.

Written by heynuff for this design demonstration.
21 October 2026

Your first aesthetic consultation is a chance to explain what you have in mind and understand the options. You might be considering surgery, a skin treatment or simply wondering where to begin. You don't need to arrive with a procedure already chosen.

The useful outcome is a clearer picture of the next step, including what still needs assessing. Here is a practical way to prepare for that conversation.

Start with what matters to you

Describe the concern in your own words. What would you like to change? What would you prefer to keep? If something about a treatment worries you, bring that up too.

Write down your questions before the appointment. A short list makes it easier to remember the things that matter when you're talking through unfamiliar options. Ask for an explanation whenever a term or recommendation is unclear. ASPS's consultation tips also encourage preparing concerns and questions in advance.

Bring the information your clinician needs

Be ready to discuss your medical history, allergies, medicines and supplements, plus any previous cosmetic treatments. Make a note beforehand if you're likely to forget a name or date.

Ask the clinic whether it needs anything else before you arrive or join the call. Give health information through the process the clinic specifies. The request form in this demo is only a preview; it is not a place to send medical records.

For skin consultations, the ASPS consultation guidance describes discussing goals, health history, medicines and previous treatments as part of the assessment.

Choose in clinic or by video call

An in-clinic appointment gives the clinician an opportunity to examine the relevant area in person. A video consultation can be a starting point for discussing your goals, history and questions. Ask the clinic what it can assess remotely and whether you will also need an in-person visit.

For a video appointment, confirm how you will receive the joining instructions. Have a phone, tablet or computer with a working camera and microphone, an internet connection and a quiet, private, well-lit space. These are also the practical basics in the NHS video-consultation guide.

Check the link and any instructions before the scheduled time. Ask the clinic who to contact if you cannot connect. The exact platform and arrangements depend on the clinic; selecting Video call in this demo does not create a real appointment or joining link.

Ask about the whole plan

Understanding an option means more than knowing its name. Useful questions include:

  • Why are you suggesting this option for my concern?
  • What alternatives should I consider, including having no treatment?
  • What results are realistic, and what are the limits?
  • What risks or complications should I understand?
  • What preparation, recovery time and follow-up might be involved?
  • What is included in the cost, and what could be additional?
  • What training and qualifications are relevant, and how can I check them?
  • Who should I contact if I have concerns afterwards?

If surgery is being discussed, ask where it would take place and who would provide the anaesthesia. The ASPS consultation checklist is another useful prompt for questions about the surgeon, procedure and aftercare.

Leave with a next step you understand

Before the conversation ends, ask what happens next. You may need another assessment, more information or time to think. If you are considering treatment, ask for the proposed plan, costs and preparation in writing.

You can review the information, raise further questions or seek another opinion before deciding. A consultation request is a step towards understanding your options; it does not mean you've agreed to treatment.

At Aurelis, the demo lets you explore a request for Makati, BGC or a video consultation. To compare the available information first, explore the treatment guide. To see the proposed appointment process, read how consultations work.
